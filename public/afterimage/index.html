Before you start

This app shows one high-contrast image and then cuts to a bright field. There is no strobing or rapid flashing, but if you have photosensitive epilepsy or migraine with aura, use Reduced intensity or skip it.

Turn your screen brightness up, sit at a normal distance, and keep your eyes on the dot. Press Esc to stop at any time.

What is actually happening / opponent process, honestly

Your retina feeds three cone types into opponent channels: roughly red-versus-green, blue-versus-yellow, and light-versus-dark. Hold a strong, steady colour on one patch of retina and the cells feeding those channels adapt — their response drifts toward a new baseline, so the constant signal stops being reported as strongly.

Cut to a neutral field and the balance is briefly wrong in the opposite direction. Green-adapted retina now reports a reddish signal from neutral light. Bright-adapted retina reports dark. That mismatch is the afterimage: an image with the lightness and the opponent axes flipped, which is exactly the plate you were staring at, flipped back.

So the plate is built by inverting in Oklab, a perceptual space: lightness is percentile-normalised and inverted inside a safe band (never true black, never paper white, so nothing crushes or bleaches), the a and b opponent axes are negated and scaled up, and the result is pulled back into the display gamut by reducing chroma rather than clipping channels — clipping would shift hue and the ghost would come back the wrong colour. A plain 255 − x RGB inversion skips all of that and gives a noticeably weaker, muddier percept.

The barely-visible drift during the stare is deliberate. A perfectly stationary retinal image fades on its own (Troxler fading); sub-pixel motion keeps edges alive without pulling your gaze. Blinking right after the cut usually strengthens the percept.

This is an ordinary, temporary property of normal vision. It fades in seconds, it is not a vision test, it does not measure or diagnose anything, and how strongly you see it says nothing about you. If a ghost lingers unusually long or you get a headache, stop.

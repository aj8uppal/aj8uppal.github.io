Adaptive Allocation: a rules-based portfolio that made money in 87% of rolling years

After seven research passes over nine built-in strategies, the one that best satisfies "more often than not makes money" is a monthly momentum-and-trend allocation across nine asset-class ETFs with a half-portfolio crash canary. It is now trading on a paper account with $100,000.

The rules

  1. Universe. SPY, QQQ, IWM (US equities), EFA, EEM (international), VNQ (real estate), GLD (gold), TLT, IEF (Treasuries). Defensive asset: IEF. Canaries: EEM and AGG.
  2. Score, monthly. Each asset's score is the average of its 3-, 6- and 12-month returns (63, 126 and 252 trading days).
  3. Eligibility. Score above zero and close above the 200-day moving average.
  4. Selection and weights. Hold the four highest scores, weighted by inverse 63-day volatility, capped at 50% each. An empty slot is not redistributed; it goes to the defensive asset.
  5. Defensive asset. Unused weight goes to IEF, but only while IEF's own score is positive. Otherwise it stays in cash. This is what kept 2022 to a 6% loss while bonds fell 13%.
  6. Canary. If EEM or AGG has a negative score, half of the risk budget goes defensive for the month.
  7. Tranches. The portfolio is four equal sleeves rebalanced roughly a week apart, so the result does not hinge on the calendar day the rules happen to run.

Everything above runs in the app unchanged: the same code produced the backtest and places the paper orders after each close.

Timing luck: rebalance a few days later

The single-tranche version looked better on month-end dates than it deserved. Shifting the rebalance by a handful of trading days exposed drawdowns of 27% and 32%, because a mid-month rebalance in autumn 2008 or early 2020 bought before the canary had turned. Splitting the portfolio into four staggered tranches removes that dependence and is why the shipped defaults quote a 20% drawdown rather than 14%.

Other checks on the canary variant

Costs
5 bps slippage: 8.6% CAGR. 15 bps: 8.0%. 30 bps: 7.1%, still 89% of rolling years positive. About 160 round trips in 19 years.
Start date
Starting in 2007, 2008, 2009, 2011, 2014 or 2018 gives 92% to 96% of rolling years positive and 12% to 14% max drawdown (single tranche).
Canary choice
EEM+AGG, VWO+BND, EEM alone, AGG alone and SPY+AGG all land within 1.3 points of CAGR and 6 points of the annual hit rate.
Defensive asset
IEF best. SHY costs 1.5 points of CAGR. Cash costs 2 points and drops the monthly hit rate to 41%. TLT doubles the drawdown.
Trend gate
Removing the 200-day filter changes nothing material (8.9% vs 8.6% CAGR), which means the momentum score is doing the work.

A decade the rules never saw: 1997 to 2007

The ETFs only go back to 2004-2007, so every number above comes from one 19-year sample. To test the rules on an era no decision touched, I swapped each ETF for a mutual fund with the same exposure (VFINX for SPY, RYOCX for QQQ, NAESX for IWM, VGTSX for EFA, VEIEX for EEM, VGSIX for VNQ, gold futures for GLD, VUSTX for TLT, VFITX for IEF, VBMFX for AGG) and ran the identical rules from June 1997. The unseen decade is the best decade, which is the pattern you want from a trend and momentum system: it earns its keep in bear markets, in this case by rotating into REITs, gold and Treasuries in 2000 to 2002 while the index lost money three years running. What was rejected along the way

Single-symbol systems on SPY (moving-average crossovers, RSI-2 pullbacks, Donchian breakouts, MACD) all had lower Sharpe ratios than buy and hold or spent 90% of the time in cash. Mean reversion had the highest win rate per trade (75% to 79%) but made 1% to 2% a year. The Faber 200-day trend filter on SPY was respectable (8.8% CAGR, 26% drawdown) but whipsawed in 2022 for a 22% loss. Sector rotation with a bond fallback got close (Sharpe 0.88) but with 20% drawdowns and no diversification outside equities.

Weekly rebalancing, a weekly canary check, and a full-portfolio canary were tested and made the result worse or narrower. All of the run logs and result tables are in the repository under research/.

On paper now

The strategy was deployed to its own paper account in the app on 2 September 2026 with $100,000. On its first run it bought QQQ, IWM, EFA and SPY (its four highest scores; gold, real estate and Treasuries did not qualify). It re-evaluates after every close and rebalances one tranche at a time. Open the Strategies tab to watch it, or the Dashboard with that account selected.

The claim being tested on paper is narrow: over the next year, does the live account track the backtest's behaviour, including its losses? A paper record that matches the simulation is the evidence that would justify real money, not the backtest itself.
